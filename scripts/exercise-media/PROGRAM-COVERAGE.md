# Program-first video delivery

Program coverage is the immediate milestone. Audit every manifest in `src/assets/programms/index.json`, recursively collecting exercise IDs from all weeks, days and native structures. Prefer resolved IDs when present. Count an exercise once per program, so repeated weeks and snapshots do not inflate priority. Missing exercises used by the most programs come first; preserve separate catalog IDs and media mappings.

`node scripts/exercise-media/program-coverage.mjs --write` saves all exercise-to-program associations, per-program gaps and priorities in `.exercise-media-references/program-coverage.json`. Add `--details` to print the full report. Missing public files, rejected mapped versions and unknown catalog IDs remain gaps. Every import refreshes this report. `catalog.mjs jobs` and `montage` use the live priority order without modifying the frozen reference inventory.

New imports and replacement versions wait for `review.json` to record `sourceSheetInspected: true`, so a clip finishing encoding during another batch's import cannot enter the app before inspection. Existing legacy draft mappings remain available while a valid replacement waits; explicitly rejected mappings are removed.

After each batch, inspect the report. As soon as all program exercises are covered, run `node scripts/exercise-media/verify-delivery.mjs --programs` to decode and compare every program-used video and poster against its source. Report this milestone immediately after that gate passes. This verifies program coverage separately from full-library delivery: continue the full library afterward unless the user changes scope. Assets remain labeled AI drafts; technical checks and contact-sheet inspection do not approve biomechanics.

For a controlled, reversible movement whose source poses are usable but out of order, a new video version may reuse the unchanged source sheet with an explicit `poseSequence` in its job: sixteen zero-based cell indices. Preserve the original version, record derivation and the sequencing operation in the new `generation.json`, and inspect the resulting video timeline. Encoding writes untouched source crops to `frames/`, the selected order to `timeline/` and `timeline.json`, and the sequence to export metadata. The verifier compares every selected cell to its source crop. Do not reverse ballistic or traveling actions. A newly generated sheet must not inherit an old sheet's sequence; queuing a revision clears it.

When repeated reference-based generation drifts at equipment or floor contacts, a calculated vector pose guide can supplement the photographs before image generation. Keep the guide and its coordinates/provenance privately alongside the reference inputs; use the built-in image generator for the final raster pose sheet. For example, the standing ring-rollout guide fixes forefoot/anchor coordinates and computes hand positions from constant strap and arm lengths. Inspect the generated sheet independently: following a guide does not certify the generated anatomy, contact geometry or motion.

Inspect the whole working range before import, including the highest overhead pose and both ends of a loop. Hold clips with disappearing weights, duplicate rings, changing strap lengths, clipped heads/weights, wrong variants or clearly misplaced muscle overlays for revision. Calf overlays belong on the rear lower leg; keep front shins gray when the calves are hidden by a front camera. For a ring muscle-up, the athlete rises relative to stationary rings: a fixed camera must not move the rings downward or lengthen the straps to keep the athlete centered. A rig-only guide can constrain that layout, but its generated result still needs inspection.

For medicine-ball clips, follow the single ball through every release, flight, catch and handoff. A backward scoop throw must travel behind the athlete; its recovery must show physical retrieval rather than a reversed throw or a ball reset. Partner full twists need two distinct handoffs on opposite sides, with the ball supported by hands at each transfer. Inspect muscle colors again when the athlete turns: an abdominal patch must not migrate onto the lower back. A color-only revision still requires a fresh pose and contact review before import.

For unilateral flyes, inspect the elbow angle through the open endpoint: a deep elbow bend can turn a generated flye into a press or pullover. A fixed elevated foot-end camera can make the lateral shoulder arc clearer. When a target surface is genuinely hidden (for example anterior quadriceps during a prone half-locust), leave it gray instead of relocating its color onto the visible hip or hamstrings; retain accurate visible secondary highlights and the app muscle-group component.

Keep kettlebell lift variants distinct during review: jerks require a second receiving knee bend, push presses a single dip and drive, and split lifts an overhead split catch followed by foot recovery before lowering. Inspect the actual encoded overhead peak for a clipped fist or bell. A framing edit may introduce an extra repetition, so recheck the entire timeline after every edit. Count legs and feet as well as hands and weights.

For persistent variant failures, remove misleading supporting photos from the next generation and supply an original guide that includes the missing constraint. A shallow bench guide also needs machine levers if the target is a machine press; a pure side camera can hide a barbell and its second grip. Inspect hand ownership after each pass-through handoff, rather than only the moment hands meet. Overhead partner-twist guides must show opposite-facing feet as well as opposite ball positions: top/bottom of the floor plan must not become overhead/under-leg passing. Keep rejected sources private and preserve each version.

Open-palm kettlebell cleans must visibly support the bell body on the requested one or two open palms, then recatch its handle before lowering; an ordinary handle-gripped rack is a different variant. For one-arm slams, follow the same ball through release, downward flight, ground contact, rebound and same-hand catch. A side ring muscle-up guide can expose the transition and retain fixed rings: include real floor clearance at the lowest hang, and compare ring positions between encoded hang/support frames despite torso occlusion.

For Pallof variants, review the lower body separately from the shoulder turn: standard presses resist rotation, while the rotation variant turns above a forward-facing pelvis and planted feet. A successful arm/torso edit can still rotate the shorts, hip joints or knees. For overhead stretches, inspect fingertips at the highest encoded pose and retain a sustained hold in the selected timeline; preserve unchanged source crops when adjusting hold duration.

For plate pinch holds, inspect an enlarged loaded hand: fingers and opposing thumb must contact the outer smooth plate faces, with no center-hole hook or floating thumb. Preserve one working hand throughout a sustained hold unless a transfer is actually shown. For seated plate twists, verify both anatomical sides rather than two repeated same-side cycles; controlled return retiming may omit accidental extra twists. For ankle strap stretches, distinguish inversion from eversion and inspect the specified heel clearance separately. Hidden deep posterior muscles must not become broad front-shin highlights.

Olympic lift drafts need the named catch: cleans turn elbows forward into front rack, snatches catch with locked arms overhead, and jerks show the second knee bend beneath locked arms. Power catches stay above parallel. A selected forward timeline may omit overly deep poses if an inspected above-parallel locked-arm pose exists; record the exact sequence and sparse-transition limitation. Do not reverse ballistic turnover or landing. Block variants retain both stationary block tops and return contacts. Strongman loading platforms must keep their world position and dimensions throughout placement; a box sliding toward the implement is grounds to hold the clip.

For sled sprints, track near/far legs separately through a whole cycle and confirm sled/body forward travel. Original side-view guides with distinct leg shades can resolve repeated-same-leg output; retain fixed sled dimensions and hand contacts. Do not manufacture visible calf highlights on ambiguous front-shin surfaces. For pull-ups, inspect the enlarged peak relative to the fixed bar and keep enough headroom for the raised endpoint. Rear-view biceps are hidden; rear upper-arm patches must not stand in for them. Alternating side planks must change anatomical support hands while retaining the appropriate floor anchors, not jump a support palm to the other hand’s former position.

For forward box sequences, an original fixed-world guide can preserve floor-to-box-to-floor travel while retaining the same box coordinates and enough overhead hand clearance. Inspect the exported highest-flight frame as well as the sheet. Pyramid is a sustained pelvis-on-ball hold: use supported settling/hold poses rather than repeated bowing or ball size drift. Rack delivery starts with scarecrow upper arms approximately parallel to the floor before elbow turnover into front rack; avoid turning it into a low upright row or clean.

Reverse-band variants retain overhead anchors: assistance bands lengthen as the bar lowers and shorten on ascent. Distinguish conventional outside-knee grips from sumo inside-knee grips. Front views must not paint hidden lumbar muscles onto the anterior waist. For recumbent bikes, use an original opposed-pedal fixed-radius guide and inspect the rendered crank separately; the guide alone does not validate occluded foot contacts or exact machine dimensions. Reverse curl previews must retain pronated grips rather than morphing into supinated curls.

Equivalent entries may reuse one inspected source only after confirming identical exercise instructions, primary/secondary muscle schema and reference-photo hashes. Record the actual generation prompt/inputs and explicit derivation; retain separate asset mappings. If only the muscle schema differs for the same referenced variant, use a built-in color edit of the inspected source and re-inspect motion/contacts/colors. Do not preserve hallucinated extra machine plates merely to make an entry unique. For assisted underhand pulls, inspect enlarged palm/knuckle orientation against an actual underhand reference and keep the knee loop attached through the full lift.

Inspect full overhead fingertips in jumping assets after any framing edit; preserve forward launch and landing rather than reverse playback. In ring dips, keep ring heights and strap lengths fixed while the body descends, and inspect the exported lowest pose for toe clearance separately from the full contact sheet. Rhomboid overlays belong to medial upper-thoracic surfaces between shoulder blades; do not use long lower-back or lateral-lat strips as a substitute. Centered carry walk cycles need explicit gait/world-translation review even after source/encoding checks pass.

For rope/cable clips, retain the entire working pulley and highest hand position inside each cell. Trace a single rope through its grips, foot lock or floor pass; a framing revision still requires a new movement-order check. Rocky pull-ups alternate front and behind-neck endpoints around the same fixed bar: a second lower bar is a mechanical failure. A seated rotary torso preview must keep seat, pelvis, knees and feet stable while the upper torso and chest-pad assembly rotate together. For rowing, inspect legs-first drive and arms-first recovery, and retain the full flywheel and rail in frame.

If a wrong raster continues to override an original alternating-leg guide, omit that raster from the next generation. A guide-only render with distinct near/far leg shades can preserve leg ownership; inspect both actual opposite peaks before accepting. Keep SVG coordinates/provenance privately with the references. For equivalent rowing variants whose reference-photo hashes match but target muscle schema differs, adapt the inspected motion through a built-in color edit and record derivation; hidden calves remain gray in front views.

For supported elbow machines, original side-view guides can hold shoulder, upper arm, pad and elbow/axle fixed while the forearm rotates. Inspect that the generator renders a human forearm and gripping hand separate from the machine link: guide lines must not become a mechanical replacement limb. A hand sliding along the lever is grounds to hold the sheet. A controlled reversible arc may be adapted through a built-in muscle-color edit and explicit extension-first pose sequencing; retain source provenance and inspect both grip endpoints and muscle surfaces again.
